Where the rates come from

The converter uses the official rates of the Bank of Russia. The Central Bank sets them on business days, so the numbers change about once a day. The “Refresh rates” button requests the data again, but the result will only change after a new rate is published.

Rates at exchange offices and banks differ from the official one: they add their own margin on buying and selling. The converter is fine for working out roughly how much something is, but for a real transaction check your bank’s rate.

General formula: amount × (rubles per source currency) ÷ (rubles per target currency). This is how our converter works too: everything is converted to rubles first, then to the currency you need.

Interesting facts

The ₽ sign

The Bank of Russia approved the graphic symbol of the ruble in December 2013. Before that it was simply abbreviated as “rub.”

Euro

The euro appeared as a non-cash currency on 1 January 1999, and banknotes and coins entered circulation on 1 January 2002.

Pound sterling

It is considered the oldest currency still in circulation: the pound’s history goes back more than a thousand years.

Tenge

Kazakhstan introduced its own currency on 15 November 1993. That day is celebrated as National Currency Day.

Face value

The Central Bank quotes the yen and the tenge per 100 units, because one unit is worth too little. The converter handles this for you.

Spread

Banks and exchange offices buy currency below the official rate and sell it above. The gap between the two is called the spread.
